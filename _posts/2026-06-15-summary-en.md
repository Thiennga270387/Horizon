## [Rio de Janeiro's 'homegrown' LLM appears to be a merge of an existing model](https://github.com/nex-agi/Nex-N2/issues/4) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The municipality of Rio de Janeiro has released a new LLM named Rio-3.5-Open-397B, claiming it to be a homegrown model. However, community analysis suggests it may actually be a weighted merge of existing models, specifically around 60% from Nex-N2 Pro and 40% from Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. This situation raises significant concerns about model attribution and originality in the AI/ML community, potentially impacting trust in AI developments. The implications of such practices could affect how future models are perceived and utilized in various applications. The community has pointed out that the model's weights are nearly identical across all layers, suggesting a lack of originality. Additionally, there are concerns regarding the absence of on-policy distillation in the uploaded model, which could affect its claimed improvements.

hackernews · unrvl22 · Jun 14, 15:37 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48528371)

**Background**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced AI systems trained on vast amounts of text data to understand and generate human-like language. The process of merging models involves combining weights from different models to create a new model that retains the strengths of its predecessors. Proper attribution is crucial in AI development to ensure credit is given to original creators and to maintain ethical standards.

**Discussion**: Community members have expressed concerns about the lack of proper attribution and the implications of merging existing models without disclosure. Some have noted the technical aspects of how the weights are combined, while others are worried about the ethical implications of such practices.
